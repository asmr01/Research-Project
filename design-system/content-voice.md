# Content & voice

The visual system makes a deck *look* right; this makes it *land*. These are the writing rules
distilled from the reference deck — the "language that resonates." Every example below is
pulled verbatim from that deck.

The through-line: **lead with the takeaway, back it with facts, and say it in the fewest precise
words.**

---

## The 12 rules

### 1. The headline is a takeaway, not a topic

Every content slide's headline is a **full declarative sentence stating the conclusion** — the
thing you'd want the reader to remember if they saw nothing else. Then the body proves it.

- ✅ *"New York is investing $1B+ in CGT — and MSHS already shares GMP capacity through the
  Empire State Consortium."*
- ✅ *"A true manufacturer is defined by in-house GMP for vectors or cells, separating them from
  delivery-only centers."*
- ❌ "New York market overview"
- ❌ "Definition of a manufacturer"

**Test:** could someone disagree with your headline? If not, it's a topic label — rewrite it as
a claim.

### 2. One idea per slide, and the headline *is* that idea

If you need two headlines, you need two slides. The eyebrow says where you are; the headline says
what to conclude; everything else is support.

### 3. Quantify everything

Numbers are the evidence. Dollars, counts, dates, named organizations — always the specific
figure over the vague adjective.

- ✅ *"~12 AMCs manufacture today"* · *"delivers 18 of 38 FDA-approved CGTs"* ·
  *"CHOP $35M · Cincinnati $60M"* · *"scaled from ~15–20 to 35–40 open trials."*
- ❌ "many centers" · "a large majority" · "significant investment"

Use `~` for estimates and `+` for floors (`$1B+`) — precision about *im*precision reads as
honest, not sloppy.

### 4. Define jargon inline, once

Spell out an acronym or term the first time, in parentheses, then use it freely.

- ✅ *"In-house GMP (Good Manufacturing Practice) is the bar that separates a manufacturer from a
  delivery-only center."*
- ✅ *"compresses vein-to-vein time from weeks to days"* (names the metric in plain terms).

### 5. Pair a term with a gloss using an em-dash

The fastest way to teach a concept: label, dash, plain-language meaning.

- ✅ *"Vectors — the delivery vehicle"* · *"Cells — the living drug."*

### 6. Frame tradeoffs as "X vs Y"

Decisions are clearer as poles than as prose.

- ✅ *"Autologous vs Allogeneic — one batch per patient (today's CAR-T; no scale economies) vs
  off-the-shelf donor batches (emerging; better economics)."*
- ✅ *"Central vs Point-of-care"* · *"Trial vs Commercial Products."*

### 7. Give the deck a numbered spine

Eyebrows run `01 ·`, `02 ·`, `03 ·` … and the executive summary's Contents mirrors them. The
reader is never lost.

### 8. Be honest about what you don't know

Flag gaps explicitly. A visible gap builds more trust than a confident guess.

- ✅ *"Limited information available — facility under construction; figure not public."*
- ✅ *"team size not public"* · *"[TBD – Current Diligence Focus]."*

### 9. Close a section with the "so-what"

After laying out facts, land a synthesis line that says what they mean for the reader.

- ✅ *"Manufacturing concentrates upstate and on Long Island, but NYC's AMCs supply the patient
  volume — and consortium access gives MSHS capacity without the CapEx."*
- ✅ *"…so the practical question is whether to deepen that partnership or build beyond it."*

### 10. Make recommendations parallel, named, and actionable

Options get a verb for a name and a concrete next step — never an open-ended "consider."

- ✅ `Option A · Hold` — *"Stay delivery-only… Diligence — Validate: cost of inaction vs.
  competitive risk."*
- ✅ `Option C · Build` — *"Stand up MSHS in-house GMP… Diligence — Validate: capex & site,
  demand model, talent plan, regulatory path."*

### 11. Classify with pills, not sentences

Categorical judgments (`Manufacturer`, `Frontier`, `Delivery only`, `Via consortium`) belong in
pills, not paragraphs. It lets the eye scan the landscape in one pass.

### 12. Cite as you go

Every fact-bearing slide ends with an 8 pt `Sources:` line; the deck closes with full URLs. Name
the source, keep it small, never skip it.

## Voice qualities

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Active verbs: *owns, shapes, compresses, anchors* | Nominalizations: *"the utilization of…"* |
| Short, load-bearing sentences | Run-ons stacking three clauses of hedging |
| Specific nouns: *Kymriah, Luxturna, CHOP* | Vague nouns: *"certain products," "some centers"* |
| Confident claims backed by a figure | Adjective inflation: *"transformative, cutting-edge"* |
| Em-dashes and `·` middots for rhythm | Walls of bullet text at uniform weight |
| Plain English for hard science | Jargon used before it's defined |

---

## The slide-writing checklist

Before a content slide is done, confirm:

- [ ] The **headline is a sentence with a conclusion**, not a topic.
- [ ] There is **one idea**, and the headline is it.
- [ ] Every claim carries a **number or named source**.
- [ ] New **jargon is defined** the first time it appears.
- [ ] Categorical calls are in **pills**, tradeoffs are **X vs Y**.
- [ ] Unknowns are **flagged**, not hidden.
- [ ] A **sources line** and **page number** are present.
- [ ] The slide could be understood from the **headline alone**.

If a reader skimmed only the headlines top to bottom, they would get the entire argument. That
is the bar.
