Content & voice

Lead with the takeaway, back it with facts

The visual system makes a deck look right; the writing makes it land. If a reader skimmed only the headlines, they would get the whole argument.

✓ Do

  • Write headlines as conclusions: "New York is investing $1B+ in CGT — and MSHS already shares GMP capacity."
  • Quantify everything: ~12 AMCs · 18 of 38 · CHOP $35M · Cincinnati $60M.
  • Define jargon inline: "GMP (Good Manufacturing Practice)."
  • Frame tradeoffs as X vs Y: Autologous vs Allogeneic; Central vs Point-of-care.
  • Flag unknowns: "Limited information available — figure not public."

✕ Don't

  • Label the topic: "New York market overview."
  • Hide behind vague scale: "many centers," "significant investment."
  • Use an acronym before it's defined.
  • Bury a judgment in prose when a pill would classify it at a glance.
  • Assert a fact with no source line at the foot of the slide.

The spine

Six rules the whole system rests on

Takeaway, not topic

The headline is a full sentence stating the conclusion. Everything below it is evidence.

One idea per slide

If you need two headlines, you need two slides.

Indigo + cyan

Two colors carry the slide; one call-out accent, maximum. Body is ink, never black.

The fixed frame

Eyebrow → headline → cyan rule → content → sources + page number. Every content slide.

A numbered spine

Eyebrows run 01·, 02·, 03· and the contents mirror them. The reader is never lost.

Cite as you go

Every fact-bearing slide names its sources; the deck closes with full URLs.
